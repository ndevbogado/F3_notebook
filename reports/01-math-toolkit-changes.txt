3. CORRECTIONS AND CLARIFICATIONS OF YOUR CONTENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C1  Where: p1, first bullet (gradient definition).
    Notes: "Indica hacia dónde se encuentra el máximo cambio de pendiente."
    Now:   The gradient points toward the direction of maximum increase of f.
    Why:   The slope is the magnitude of the gradient; it is not the thing that
           changes. Your own later bullet on p1 already says "dirección de
           máximo incremento", so this makes the first bullet consistent.

C2  Where: p1, bullet 3 ("radio de crecimiento").
    Now:   "tasa de crecimiento" (rate of growth).
    Why:   "Radio" (radius) looks like a slip for "ratio/rate".

C3  Where: p1, bottom "Nota".
    Notes: nabla = [d/dx ; d/dy ; d/dz] . [x^ ; y^ ; z^]  (dot between lists).
    Now:   nabla = x^ d/dx + y^ d/dy + z^ d/dz.
    Why:   It is a sum of three terms, each a unit vector times a derivative.
           As written, it reads as a scalar dot product, which is wrong for an
           operator that has to stay a vector.

C4  Where: p2, divergence formula.
    Notes: div T = dT/dx + dT/dy + dT/dz.
    Now:   div F = dFx/dx + dFy/dy + dFz/dz.
    Why:   Divergence differentiates each vector component along its own axis.
           Your margin note ("pierde carácter vectorial") shows you meant the
           components; this just writes them out.

C5  Where: p2-p3, "Nos da una noción de la velocidad con la que nos movemos
           respecto a p0".
    Now:   Divergence measures the expansion rate of the field, i.e. net flux
           out of a small neighbourhood of p0 per unit volume.
    Why:   Divergence is not a velocity of motion relative to p0. It is flux
           density. Marked "Aclarado" because the idea behind your sentence
           (how fast the field spreads) is kept.

C6  Where: p3, "Los puntos de div T <= 0 son sumideros".
    Now:   div < 0 are sinks; div = 0 is neither a source nor a sink.
    Why:   Zero divergence means zero net flux, which is not a sink.

C7  Where: p4, (A), title "Laplaciano (divergencia de la divergencia F)".
    Now:   Divergence of the gradient.
    Why:   The divergence of a divergence does not exist (the divergence of a
           vector field is a scalar, and divergence needs a vector). The
           Laplacian is div(grad f).

C8  Where: p4, (A), last equation: div(curl F) = d2T/dx2 + d2T/dy2 + d2T/dz2.
    Now:   div(grad f) = nabla^2 f = d2f/dx2 + d2f/dy2 + d2f/dz2.
    Why:   The right side is the Laplacian; the left side had a cross
           product by mistake. The line right above it in your notes (the
           product of the two brackets) is correct and only uses a gradient.

C9  Where: p4, (E).
    Notes: curl(curl T) = grad(div T) = -nabla^2 T.
    Now:   curl(curl F) = grad(div F) - nabla^2 F  (vector Laplacian).
    Why:   Correct identity has a subtraction and no second equals sign. The
           notes' version equates grad(div T) with -nabla^2 T, which is false
           in general. Verified symbolically with SymPy (see A5).

C10 Where: p4, (E), comment "No da un resultado conocido".
    Now:   It does: it is the identity from C9, used for example to derive the
           wave equation from Maxwell's equations.
    Why:   The notes' comment contradicted the identity. Also explains why (C)
           appears inside (E).

C11 Where: p2, item 1 "Prod. por escalar: alpha grad T = [alpha dT/dx; ...]".
    Now:   Clarified that nabla times a scalar field is the gradient, and that
           for a constant alpha: nabla(alpha f) = alpha nabla f (linearity).
    Why:   The item mixed "multiplying nabla by a scalar field" with "scaling
           the gradient by a constant". Content is kept, only the wording.
